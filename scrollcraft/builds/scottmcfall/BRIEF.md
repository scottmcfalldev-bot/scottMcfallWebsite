## The eight topics

1. **Vibe** *(authored)*: calm, exact, a little dry, quietly satisfying.
   References: a well-kept ledger book; the before/after spread in a
   renovation magazine; the moment a train departures board settles.
2. **Journey** *(evidence: approved plan; order authored)*: the busywork they
   recognise → what it costs them (their own numbers) → watching it leave
   their hands → proof it has been done for real teams → the price and the
   one next step → booking.
3. **Energy curve** *(authored)*: quiet open, flat and practical in the
   calculator, the only real motion on the page in the crossing, steady
   through proof, a single decisive sweep at the close, then still.
4. **Feeling, and the one moment** *(authored)*: see the feeling curve below.
   The moment: their own list of chores sliding across the line one at a time
   while the "hours by hand" number drops.
5. **What no other site does** *(authored)*: it uses the visitor's own task
   list, typed into the calculator, as the material of the page's big moment.
6. **Range** *(Scott)*: editorial, restrained.
7. **World or scenes** *(Scott)*: distinct scenes.
8. **Assets** *(Scott)*: none generated. Type, real UI and real data only.

Buyer, offer and prices are fixed by the approved plan: agencies and
consulting firms with 5 to 30 people; free bottleneck call, $2,500 first
automation, $750/mo Keep It Running, 3 founding spots at $1,500. Formation
numbers are real but estimated and must say so. No invented statistics.

## Journey

```
1  Recognition   the same update, typed five times, is their week
2  Unease        their own tasks and hours, priced per year
3  Relief        the tasks cross the line one by one (PEAK)
4  Trust         real projects, each shown as before and after
5  Resolve       the line sweeps away; one offer, one price, one action
6  Follow-through the booking calendar, the short answers, the footer
```

## Feeling curve

```
1  Recognition   a split screen: "by hand" on paper, "automated" in forest, both headlines at once
2  Unease        a plain calculator on the manual side; the yearly cost lands on the automated side
3  Relief        each task slip crosses the divider and becomes one quiet automation line; the readout counts down
4  Trust         before on the left, after on the right, wiped in per side, for five real projects
5  Resolve       the paper side folds away to nothing; the forest side holds the price and the button
6  Calm          no motion at all; a calendar and short answers on the winning ground
```

Adjacent states all differ. The act before the peak (calculator) is the
stillest interactive act on the page, so the crossing arrives from quiet.

## The peak

> "I typed in my own busywork, and when I scrolled, each task slid across the
> line and the hours-by-hand number kept dropping."

Lives in act 3. Largest span on the page (3.2 viewport-heights). If the
visitor skipped the calculator, six common agency tasks are used and the page
says plainly that they are examples.

## Tell-someone sentence

**It's the site where your own to-do list slides across a line and turns into
things that run by themselves.**

## Authored silence

None. Every act has content on screen at every scroll position. The collapse
act's first frames are deliberately the same split as before so the sweep has
something to sweep.

## Honesty rules for this build

- Hours per task are the visitor's own input, or labelled examples (2 hrs/wk).
- The 70% reduction is the site's stated estimate and is labelled as one.
- Formation figures carry "estimated".
- No counters on anything that is not computed from the visitor's input.
